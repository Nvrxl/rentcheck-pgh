      How we score this

      Only building and fire safety cases (structural problems, fire hazards, electrical and heating issues) change the rating. Trash, weeds and debris make up about half of all city records. We still list them in the report, but they earn no points, because they say little about whether an apartment is safe to live in.

      Each building or fire safety case earns points:

      • 3 points if it is still unresolved and was issued in the last 3 years.
      • 1 point if it is still unresolved but older. Cases can sit "In Court" for years, and the data can't tell us whether an old one is still a real problem.
      • 1 point for each case fixed in the last 3 years, up to 4 points in total. A history of fixed problems can raise the rating to "Some concern" but never to "High concern".

      8 or more points is "High concern", 3 to 7 is "Some concern", and fewer than 3 is "Low concern".

      These are plain rules written in code. No AI or language model is involved, and the same records always give the same rating. The cut-offs are our own judgment, so treat the rating as a starting point for your own questions, not a verdict on the landlord.

      If we find no records, we say so and never call the place "safe": the address may not have matched, problems may not have been reported, or it may be outside the City of Pittsburgh.
